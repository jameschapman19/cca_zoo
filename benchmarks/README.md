# Benchmarks

Two benchmarks of the missing-view models against MOFA+ (the method most multi-omics analyses
reach for). They are not part of the test suite or the package. They need `mofapy2`,
`matplotlib`, `pandas` and, for CLL, `rdata`:

```bash
uv pip install mofapy2 matplotlib pandas rdata
```

## `missing_views/`: simulation

Simulated views with shared and private factors and whole samples removed, completely at
random or tied to the biology, with dense and sparse loadings, in a moderate and a hard
(faint shared signal, wide views) regime. See its README for the design, the metrics and the
caveats. Three of its figures are kept in `output/`: recovery of the shared factors in each
regime, and filling in a missing view in the moderate one. The raw results and the other
figures are regenerated by `run.py` and `plot.py` (about 30 minutes for the moderate regime and
15 for the hard one on four cores) and are not committed.

In short: MOFA+ matches or beats the CCA-family models on recovering the shared factors under
heavy missingness; `IncompleteGCCA` has a modest, noisy lead at around 30% of views missing, and
is much faster. For filling in a missing view the CCA-family models are safe (never far below
predicting the mean, with the calibrated `predict` and `impute`) but MOFA+ imputes better
wherever there is real signal.

## `cll/`: the CLL cohort of the MOFA paper

200 chronic lymphocytic leukaemia patients with drug response, methylation, mRNA and somatic
mutations (Argelaguet et al., 2018), downloaded from the MOFAdata package. Drugs, methylation and
mRNA are each missing for whole patients (16, 4 and 64 of 200), which is the case these models are
built for. IGHV mutation status and trisomy 12 are columns of the mutation view; they are held out
of it and used as labels that no method sees.

```bash
python cll/run.py --out cll/output
```

The tables below come from the `results.json` it writes, which is not committed.

Every method gets 10 factors.

**Do the factors recover IGHV status and trisomy 12?** Cross-validated AUC of a logistic regression
on each method's factors (5 x 5 folds):

| Method | IGHV | trisomy12 |
|---|---|---|
| IncompleteGCCA | 0.95 | 0.93 |
| Complete cases | 0.97 | 0.94 |
| Mean-imputed | 0.96 | 0.93 |
| MOFA+ (default) | 0.98 | 0.97 |
| MOFA+ (best of 5 random) | 0.98 | 0.96 |

**Can a hidden view be filled in?** Skill over predicting the view's mean, for a quarter of the
patients who have every view, hidden at random (mean of 5 repeats; 0 is no better than the mean):

| Method | Drugs | mRNA |
|---|---|---|
| IncompleteGCCA | -0.10 | 0.05 |
| Complete cases | 0.07 | 0.04 |
| Mean-imputed | 0.04 | 0.04 |
| MOFA+ (default) | -0.15 | -0.79 |
| MOFA+ (best of 5 random) | -0.06 | -1.97 |

What this shows, and does not:

- The shared factors of every method recover IGHV status and trisomy 12 without seeing them. MOFA+
  is a little ahead (AUC 0.98 on IGHV and 0.96-0.97 on trisomy 12, against 0.95 and 0.93 for
  `IncompleteGCCA`), and fitting on
  the 121 complete patients alone does as well as using all 200, so on this cohort using the
  incomplete patients does not help the recovery of these labels.
- Filling in a hidden view is safe with `IncompleteGCCA` and not much better than the mean: the other
  views carry little linear signal about drug response beyond it. MOFA+ can fall far below the mean,
  most of all on mRNA.
- One cohort, two labels, five repeats. It replicates the biology; it does not show an accuracy
  advantage over MOFA+.
